# X-01 · Working in a client environment

- **Where it sits:** Cross-track overlay
- **Who:** Required: all engineering roles, all product and experience roles, management, business development · Optional: HR, others
- **Delivery:** Overlay: 10 min added to any track session; required reading before client work
- **Time:** 10 min

People who work inside a client's systems are bound by the client's rules first. A habit that is fine at home can be a contract breach on site.

## Outcomes

A participant can:

- State the order of authority between client rules, organization rules and personal preference.
- Ask the right questions on the first day of an engagement.
- Apply the working method even where AI tools are not allowed.

## Topics

### Required

- Order of authority: the client's contract and policy, then the organization's policy, then personal preference. If the client says no AI, it is no AI.
- First-day questions: which tools are approved, which data and code may they process, on whose accounts and licences, is disclosure required, who owns AI-assisted output, are there logging or audit requirements.
- Client code, data and documents never go into a tool the client has not approved. That includes the tools of your own organization.
- No personal accounts. No carrying context from one engagement to another.
- When in doubt, ask the client lead in writing and keep the answer.

### Recommended

- Bringing practices into a client repository (instruction files, skills): propose, do not impose.
- Where AI is forbidden, the method still applies: clear specifications, small changes, explicit gates.
- Wording for disclosure.
- AI attribution in commits follows the client's policy.
- At handover, instruction files and specifications are documented assets.

### Optional

- A checklist of contract clauses: intellectual property, confidentiality, AI use.
- Client enablement as a service.

## Checkpoint

> Your organization approves a tool; the client has not mentioned it. May you paste the client's code into it?

## Practice

Write the five questions you would send to a client lead before starting.

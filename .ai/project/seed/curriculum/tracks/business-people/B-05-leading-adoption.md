# B-05 · Leading adoption

- **Where it sits:** Business and people track
- **Who:** Required: management · Recommended: HR, business development · Optional: architects and tech leads, others
- **Delivery:** Business and people track, leadership session; also usable self-serve
- **Time:** 25 min

Training changes individuals. Adoption needs decisions only leadership can make: which tools, what rules, what gets measured, and what is said to clients and to staff worried about their roles.

## Outcomes

A participant can:

- List what has to be in place before asking people to use AI at work.
- Choose measures that reward outcomes instead of activity.
- Address role and skill concerns directly.

## Topics

### Required

- In place first: approved tools and plans, a usage policy, data classification, a named owner, a place for shared practices.
- Expectations: AI-assisted work meets the same quality bar, and accountability is unchanged.
- Measure outcomes (cycle time, rework, quality, satisfaction), not prompt counts or lines generated.
- Address role concerns openly: skills shift toward specifying, reviewing and deciding, and learning time is budgeted.
- A client-facing position: disclosure, contract terms, what the organization commits to ([X-01](../../overlays/X-01-working-in-a-client-environment.md)).

### Recommended

- Stages of adoption: individual experiments, shared practices, governed workflows.
- A community of practice: champions, office hours, show-and-tell.
- Selecting and reviewing pilots.
- Budget: licences, usage-based costs, training time.
- Developing junior staff and the risk of skills not being built.
- Effects on hiring and onboarding.

### Optional

- Governance frameworks.
- Return-on-investment models.
- Vendor strategy and lock-in.
- AI in the service offering and its pricing.

## Checkpoint

> Which would you report to the leadership team next quarter: number of prompts, licences activated, or rework rate on delivered work? Why?

## Practice

Fill in a one-page adoption plan: owner, approved tools, three measures, first pilot, review date.

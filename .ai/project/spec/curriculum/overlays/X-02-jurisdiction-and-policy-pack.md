# X-02 · Jurisdiction and policy pack

- **Where it sits:** Cross-track overlay
- **Who:** Everyone: required
- **Delivery:** Overlay: one slide in the core session; the pack itself is a reference document
- **Time:** 2 min in session

Privacy law, language requirements and the approved-tool list differ by place and by organization. Keeping them in one replaceable pack lets the rest of the program be delivered anywhere.

## Outcomes

A participant can:

- Find the organization's rule for a given tool and data class.
- Name the local legal topics that touch AI-assisted work.
- Know who to ask.

## Topics

### Required

- The pack fills the placeholders the core modules point to: the approved-tool list, the data classes with a "what goes where" matrix, the incident contact, and the local legal topics.
- Default pack, Quebec and Canada (verify at build time): Quebec's private-sector privacy law (Law 25), including consent, privacy impact assessments, transfers of personal information outside Quebec and transparency on automated decisions; the federal private-sector privacy law (PIPEDA); consent for recording; French-language requirements for workplace tools and communications; for clients with European exposure, the GDPR and the EU AI Act.
- The pack names the questions and the people to ask. It is not legal advice.

### Recommended

- A one-page matrix per organization.
- Incident reporting contacts and steps.
- Add-ons by sector: public sector, finance, health.

### Optional

- Packs for other jurisdictions.

## Checkpoint

> Where do you look to find out whether a given tool is approved for client documents?

## Presenter notes

- Have the organization's privacy lead review this pack before the first delivery. Do not present legal content they have not seen.

## Re-verify before each delivery

- All legal content, the approved-tool list and the contacts.

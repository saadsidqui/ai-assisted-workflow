# C-07 · Data, security and responsible use

- **Where it sits:** Common core
- **Who:** Everyone: required
- **Delivery:** Core session, segment 4
- **Time:** 5 min

One pasted secret or client document can undo the benefit of the whole program. People need a rule they can apply in five seconds, not a policy document.

## Outcomes

A participant can:

- Decide in five seconds whether a piece of information may go into a given tool.
- Name the settings that affect what happens to their data.
- Know what to do after a mistake.

## Topics

### Required

- Work data goes into approved tools only. A personal account is not an approved tool. A client's rules override the organization's ([X-01](../overlays/X-01-working-in-a-client-environment.md)).
- Data classes in practice: public, internal, confidential, restricted (personal information, credentials, client material, source code under agreement). One page shows what may go where ([X-02](../overlays/X-02-jurisdiction-and-policy-pack.md)).
- Never paste secrets or credentials, personal information about identifiable people, or client-confidential material into a tool that is not approved for it.
- Settings matter: training on your data, retention, sharing links, connectors, memory.
- Content can carry instructions. A document, web page, email or ticket can tell the assistant to do something (prompt injection). The more access the assistant has, the more this matters.
- Intellectual property basics: check the licence of what you bring in, do not present others' work as yours, know the client's position on AI-assisted deliverables.
- After a mistake: report it quickly. The path is blame-free and the contact is named.

### Recommended

- Least privilege for connectors and agents; read-only first.
- Recording and transcribing meetings needs consent and, with clients, their agreement first.
- Disclosure: when and how the organization says that AI assisted.
- Bias and fairness in anything that concerns people.
- Use anonymized or synthetic data for practice and demonstrations.

### Optional

- Threat modelling an AI workflow.
- Vendor assessment and data-processing agreements.
- Governance frameworks (NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001).
- Regulatory mapping for the jurisdiction ([X-02](../overlays/X-02-jurisdiction-and-policy-pack.md)).

## Checkpoint

> Five snippets on screen. For each: paste, do not paste, or it depends on the tool?

## Presenter notes

- Do not teach policy from memory. Read the organization's actual rule from the one-page matrix and show where it lives.

## Re-verify before each delivery

- Vendor data-handling terms and the organization's approved-tool list.

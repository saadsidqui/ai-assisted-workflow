# P-05 · Content and UX writing

- **Where it sits:** Product and experience track
- **Who:** Required: designers · Recommended: PM / PO, analysts
- **Delivery:** Product and experience track, session 3; also usable self-serve
- **Time:** 20 min

Readers now recognize machine-written text and discount it. The skill is editing: keeping the speed and removing the tells.

## Outcomes

A participant can:

- Produce and choose among copy variants using stated criteria.
- Recognize and remove the patterns that mark text as machine-written.
- Produce French content that is written, not translated.

## Topics

### Required

- Microcopy, labels, error messages, onboarding and help content: generate variants quickly and choose by clarity, tone, length and consistency.
- Voice and terminology: supply a style guide and a glossary, and enforce consistent terms.
- Recognizable patterns to edit out: filler openers, inflated claims, lists of three by reflex, hedging, uniform sentence rhythm, summaries that restate.
- Fact-check every claim, number and name. No invented testimonials or statistics.
- French and bilingual content: write in the target language where possible instead of translating. Check anglicisms, typography and terminology against the official terminology resources, and have a native speaker review.

### Recommended

- A content pipeline with named gates: outline, draft, edit, fact-check, publish.
- Plain-language rewriting and reading level.
- Structured, reusable content.
- A localization workflow built on the glossary.
- Documentation from source material: user guides, release notes.

### Optional

- Content governance and style packaged as skills.
- Writing for search and for AI answer engines.
- Alternative text at volume, with review.

## Checkpoint

> One paragraph on screen. Underline the three phrases that give it away as machine-written.

## Practice

Edit a generated paragraph down to what a careful human writer would have written. Compare word counts.

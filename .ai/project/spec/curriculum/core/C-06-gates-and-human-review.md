# C-06 · Gates and human review

- **Where it sits:** Common core
- **Who:** Everyone: required
- **Delivery:** Core session, segment 4
- **Time:** 5 min

Responsibility does not move to the tool. This message runs through every module; this is where it is stated plainly and turned into practice.

## Outcomes

A participant can:

- List what to verify in an AI-produced result.
- Name the points in their own work where a human approval gate belongs.
- Explain why convincing output gets checked less carefully, and what to do about it.

## Topics

### Required

- A result is a draft until someone has checked it. Nothing AI-produced leaves your hands unread.
- You sign it, you own it. Accountability stays with the person who sends, ships or decides.
- What to check: facts, numbers, names, quotes, citations and links (invented references are common), calculations, code behaviour, omissions, tone.
- Check against the original request and the source, not against how convincing it reads.
- Gates: explicit human approval before anything external, irreversible or consequential (send, publish, merge, deploy, decide about a person).
- Review effort grows with the stakes.

### Recommended

- Automation bias and review fatigue: the more fluent the output and the more of it there is, the less carefully people read. Work in smaller batches and review with fresh eyes.
- Evidence over assertion: tests that ran, sources that were opened, numbers that were recomputed. "It says it checked" is not evidence.
- For high-stakes work, the person who approves is not the person who produced. A second model can be an extra reviewer and never the only one.
- Record assumptions and approvals for important deliverables.

### Optional

- Quality rubrics and evaluation sets for recurring tasks.
- Sampling audits of AI-assisted work.
- A red-team exercise.

## Checkpoint

> Would you send this without opening the source? What would you check first?

## Practice

Hand out a short AI-written summary with two planted errors and its source. Find them in three minutes.

## Presenter notes

- State the accountability sentence in the first five minutes of the session and repeat it here. It should not arrive as a closing disclaimer.

# P-01 · Discovery and synthesis

- **Where it sits:** Product and experience track
- **Who:** Required: all product and experience roles
- **Delivery:** Product and experience track, session 1; also usable self-serve
- **Time:** 20 min

Discovery produces more raw material than anyone has time to read twice. AI is good at structuring it and equally good at inventing a tidy theme nobody expressed.

## Outcomes

A participant can:

- Turn raw notes into decisions, actions, questions and themes that trace back to their source.
- Reject a synthesized theme that has no supporting evidence.
- Handle transcripts and interview data within the rules.

## Topics

### Required

- From raw material (notes, transcripts, interviews, tickets) to structured output: decisions, actions, open questions, themes. Each item points back to where it came from.
- Ask for quotes or evidence for every theme. No evidence, no theme.
- Preparation as drafts: interview guides, workshop agendas, stakeholder maps.
- Transcripts contain personal and client information: consent first, approved tool, anonymize where possible.
- A synthesis is a hypothesis until the people involved have confirmed it.

### Recommended

- Clustering at volume, with counts and counter-examples.
- Finding contradictions across sources.
- Market and competitor scans with cited research and checked dates.
- Personas and journeys labelled as hypotheses, never as a substitute for research.
- Ask what is missing and whose voice is absent.

### Optional

- A research repository with retrieval.
- Analysis of open-text survey answers.
- Simulated users and their limits.

## Checkpoint

> The assistant found five themes. Which do you keep: the five that read well, or the ones with at least two quotes each?

## Practice

Synthesize a set of fictional interview notes into themes with quotes. One planted theme has no support; find it.
